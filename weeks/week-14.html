Explore

What makes a portfolio strong

Specific beats general. Real job postings, real numbers, real dates. Your own voice. Evidence that you did the research rather than repeated it. A clear thread from who you are (§1) to what you want (§2–3) to how you will get there (§4–5).

The elevator pitch

Sixty seconds, four parts: who you are, what you are working on, what you are looking for, and a question or ask. Practice until it sounds like conversation, not a script.

Talking about a non-traditional background

Retail, food service, the military, caregiving, healthcare, and trades all build skills IT employers want: staying calm with upset customers, following procedures, working shifts, solving problems under pressure. Name the skill, give a one-sentence example, and connect it to the role.

The STAR method

For “tell me about a time when…” questions, answer with Situation, Task, Action, Result. Keep the situation short and spend most of your time on what you did.
