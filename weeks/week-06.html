Explore

Administrator or engineer?

A network administrator runs the network that exists: monitors it, adds users and devices, fixes outages, applies updates. A network engineer designs and builds: plans new sites, chooses equipment, and solves the hardest problems. Titles overlap, so read the duties in each posting.

Sources: BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook pages for administrators (opens in a new tab) and architects (opens in a new tab). The numbers tell a story: routine administration is shrinking as automation grows, while design and cloud skills are growing.

The certification paths

CompTIA Network+ is vendor-neutral: it covers how networks work regardless of brand, and fits well after A+. Cisco certifications teach Cisco equipment, which is common in enterprise networks. Cisco’s entry credential is the CCST (Cisco Certified Support Technician) in Networking; the CCNA is the best-known associate certification and is a common requirement for network admin jobs; CCNP and CCIE come later in a career.

Cloud networking

Cloud platforms have their own virtual networks, firewalls, and load balancers, configured with code instead of cables. Network professionals who add cloud and automation skills (scripting, Ansible, infrastructure as code) are the ones whose roles are growing.

The NJ/NY market

The New York metro area has one of the largest concentrations of IT jobs in the country, especially in finance, healthcare, pharmaceuticals, telecommunications, and government. Pay in the region generally runs above the national median, but so does cost of living. Use the BLS state data or CyberSeek’s metro view to compare.
