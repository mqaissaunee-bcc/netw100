Week 11: Networking, mentorship, and the job search

Many IT jobs are filled through people who know people. Learn how to build professional relationships, ask for informational interviews, and find events and competitions near you.

Explore

Professional networking

Professional networking is building relationships with people in your field before you need anything from them. It is not collecting connections. A good relationship starts with curiosity: ask people about their work, listen, and follow up.

Informational interviews

An informational interview is a 15–20 minute conversation where you ask someone about their job. You are not asking for a job. People say yes more often than you would think, especially to students. Prepare questions, respect their time, and send a thank-you within a day.

Professional organizations

ISACA New Jersey

Audit, governance, risk, and compliance. Strong for GRC interests.

ISSA

Information Systems Security Association: chapters for security practitioners.

ISC2 chapters

Local chapters of the organization behind CC and CISSP.

OWASP

Open Worldwide Application Security Project: free meetups on web and application security.

Competitions

Capture the flag (CTF) events are security puzzles solved in teams. The National Cyber League runs seasons open to college students. The Collegiate Cyber Defense Competition (CCDC) has student teams defend networks against live attackers. CyberPatriot is for middle and high school students; college students can get involved as mentors, which is great experience to talk about in interviews.

Mentors and internships

A mentor is someone a few steps ahead who checks in with you over time. Mentors often come from informational interviews, professional organization meetings, or instructors. Internships and co-ops are the fastest way to get “experience” on a resume; Handshake and the Career Center list them.
