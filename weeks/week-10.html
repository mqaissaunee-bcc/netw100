Explore

Anatomy of an entry-level IT resume

One page. Contact info and a LinkedIn link at the top. A two-line summary aimed at the role. A skills section with tools you have actually used. Certifications (including “in progress” with a target date). Projects and labs if you lack IT jobs. Experience, including non-IT jobs written to highlight transferable skills. Education.

Bullets that work

A strong bullet starts with an action verb, says what you did, and shows a result or scale: “Resolved 40+ customer issues per shift at a busy retail service desk, keeping satisfaction above 95%.” Weak bullets list duties: “Responsible for customer service.”

LinkedIn

Your headline is the line under your name. Make it about where you are heading: “Network & IT student at Brookdale | CompTIA A+ in progress | Help desk ready.” Your About section is a short, friendly version of your bio. Add skills recruiters search for, and keep a clear, professional photo.

Proof of skill

No experience? Build evidence. A home lab, a GitHub repository with scripts or lab write-ups, and a public TryHackMe profile showing completed rooms all count. A short write-up of what you did and learned matters more than the badge.

Common mistakes

Two pages of unrelated detail. Listing every tool you have heard of. Typos. An email like gamerkid2007@. A generic resume sent to every job. Social media posts you would not want an employer to see.
