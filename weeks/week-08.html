Explore

Cloud roles

Cloud engineer

Builds and runs systems on Azure, AWS, or Google Cloud, usually by writing code that creates the infrastructure.

Cloud security engineer

Secures cloud accounts, identities, networks, and data. Hunts for misconfigurations, the most common cause of cloud breaches.

DevSecOps engineer

Builds security checks into the pipeline that turns developers’ code into running software.

Shared responsibility

In the cloud, the provider secures the physical data centers, hardware, and core platform. The customer secures what it puts there: accounts, data, settings, and access. Most cloud breaches come from the customer side, such as a storage bucket left public or an account without MFA.

Identity and access management (IAM)

IAM decides who can sign in and what each person can reach. The principle of least privilege says everyone gets only the access their job needs. As networks spread across clouds and home offices, identity has become the new perimeter, and IAM specialists are in demand.

Privacy and compliance

GDPR is the European Union’s privacy law; it applies to organizations that handle EU residents’ personal data, including many U.S. companies. HIPAA protects health information in the U.S. PCI DSS is the security standard for organizations that handle payment cards. Knowing which rules apply is everyday GRC work.

AI’s impact

CyberSeek’s 2025 data found that about 10 percent of cybersecurity job listings specifically mention AI skills. AI tools already help SOC analysts summarize alerts and help attackers write convincing phishing. Routine tasks are being automated, while judgment, communication, and the ability to check AI output are becoming more valuable. Brookdale has proposed a 200-level course, AI Cybersecurity Foundations, that goes deeper.
